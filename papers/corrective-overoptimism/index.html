Corrective Overoptimism

Author: Brandon S.C. Chan (Department of Economics, Yale University, 2026)

Links: Manuscript PDF (48 pp.) · Online Appendix PDF (19 pp.) · Replication Appendix PDF (5 pp.) · Technical Digest

Abstract

Booms in infrastructure investment — railways in the 1840s, electricity in the 1920s, fibre in the 1990s — often coincide with investor exuberance and losses. But the infrastructure remains long after these episodes. I show that investor overoptimism can serve as a second-best corrective force against chronic underinvestment: when private promoters cannot appropriate the network, knowledge, and allocative spillovers of connective infrastructure, diagnostic expectations over-extrapolate good news and induce irreversible investments, pushing capital accumulation toward the socially efficient benchmark. This mechanism — corrective overoptimism — is embedded in a model of spatial development with endogenous infrastructure and estimated on data from England and Wales, 1700–1881, spanning the canal and railway booms. Corrective overoptimism can be a potent channel of growth.
